    How the score and confidence work, and what to tune for this spot

    Every daylight hour gets up to four independent estimates of the same thing, each 0 to 10: energy score × wind factor × tide factor. The combined score is their average. Confidence comes from how much they disagree, and from whether the reference buoy matches what the models say is there right now.

    • A, offshore model. NOAA GFS Wave at the spot's offshore reference point (the Strait entrance for the Strait spots, a cell a few miles off the beach for Westport). Up to three swell components, each direction-filtered and shifted forward by travel time to the spot.
    • B, nearshore model. Météo-France MFWAM at a grid cell next to the spot. For the Strait spots this model has already simulated the loss on the way in, so heights are much smaller and get their own thresholds.
    • C, NWS forecaster. The "Wave Detail" line for each period of the relevant NWS zone text, parsed into components and treated like A.
    • D, buoy nowcast. The latest reading from the reference buoy, shifted forward by its travel time. Only exists for the next few hours, but it is measured, not modelled.
    • Rideable face estimate. Derived. Until three logged sessions at a spot it is the nearshore model height × a placeholder factor (marked as a guess). After three sessions it becomes your own median ratio of logged face height to nearshore model height at that hour.
    • Confidence. High when the estimates at the peak hour sit within 1.5 points of each other, medium within 3, low beyond. One step lower if the buoy's aligned energy right now differs from the models' by more than a factor of two.
    • Energy index is derived: sum over swell components of (direction credit × height² × period), metres and seconds. Components shorter than the minimum period are ignored as chop.
    • Arrival lag is derived from deep-water group speed (0.78 m/s per second of period) over the straight-line distance from the reference point to the spot.
    • Wind. Under the calm threshold any direction scores full. Offshore is tolerated up to its limit, onshore least, cross wind in between.
    • Tide. Each spot has a preferred part of the cycle; hours outside it are trimmed, not zeroed.
